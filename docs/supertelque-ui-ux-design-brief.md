# SuperTelque Website UI/UX Design Brief

## 1. Project Overview

SuperTelque is a premium B2B revenue operations and AI engineering partner. The company helps ambitious B2B businesses turn fragmented CRM data, manual workflows, and disconnected GTM tools into predictable, measurable, and scalable revenue systems.

This is a services and lead-generation website, not a SaaS product dashboard. Its main purpose is to establish trust with senior decision-makers and convert qualified visitors into either a strategy-session booking or a direct contact inquiry.

### Core positioning

> We design and build the systems behind scalable B2B revenue teams.

SuperTelque combines:

- Fractional RevOps leadership
- CRM and pipeline architecture
- AI agent and workflow engineering
- GTM automation
- Demand generation
- Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)
- Data integration and reporting
- Revenue forecasting and operating systems

The strongest differentiator is:

> We are technical revenue operators, not a marketing agency.

## 2. Target Audience

The primary audience includes:

- Founders
- CROs
- Heads of RevOps
- Sales and marketing leaders
- Operations leaders
- Series A to Series C B2B companies
- SaaS, fintech, AI, manufacturing, logistics, ecommerce, healthcare, and education businesses

These visitors may be experiencing:

- Unreliable CRM data
- Conflicting forecasting systems
- Slow lead routing
- Excessive manual research and list-building
- Disconnected sales and marketing tools
- Fragmented revenue reporting
- Unclear ways to apply AI to the GTM stack
- Inconsistent demand generation
- Pressure to add headcount before fixing the underlying system

The audience is commercially sophisticated. They are not primarily looking for generic marketing services. They want better operating visibility, less manual work, stronger process control, and measurable revenue outcomes.

## 3. Conversion Goals

The website should guide visitors toward two main actions:

1. **Book a free 45-minute strategy session**
2. **Send a direct contact inquiry**

The primary conversion path is the booking flow. The contact form is a secondary path for visitors who are not ready to schedule immediately or have a more specific engagement question.

The strategy session promises:

- A review of the current revenue stack
- Identification of quick wins
- CRM health assessment
- Automation opportunities
- Growth recommendations
- A written 90-day plan

The session is positioned as having an estimated market value of $2,500 while being free for qualifying B2B teams.

## 6. Current Visual Direction

The current site uses a technical, dark, data-driven visual language.

Key characteristics:

- Deep navy backgrounds
- White content sections
- Gold and amber CTAs
- Electric blue accents
- Dashboard-style visuals
- Monospace labels
- Rounded cards
- Architecture diagrams
- Animated data flows
- Pipeline metrics
- Glassmorphism and translucent surfaces
- Scroll-triggered motion

The current visual impression is:

> Technical, systems-oriented, animated, and revenue-dashboard driven.

The current design communicates technical competence, but it is more developer-dashboard oriented than premium consultancy oriented. The redesign should preserve the precision and intelligence while making the experience calmer, more editorial, more human, and more distinctive.

## 7. Design Direction for the Redesign

The new website should feel:

- Precise
- Calm
- Expensive
- Trustworthy
- Strategic
- Technically capable
- Human
- Confident without being loud
- animated content added

It should feel like a senior partner is helping a leadership team make an important operational decision, rather than like a software platform is displaying system metrics.

### Recommended design shift

Move the emphasis from:

> Here are our tools, dashboards, and automations.

Toward:

> Here is how we remove revenue friction and create a system your team can trust.

### Visual principles

- Lead with business outcomes before technology names.
- Use technology logos as supporting evidence, not the main story.
- Use fewer generic dashboard panels and more purposeful visual storytelling.
- Make the human expertise behind the systems visible.
- Make case studies feel credible, specific, and editorial.
- Use strong typography and generous spacing to create confidence.
- Make the booking journey feel simple, valuable, and low-pressure.
- Create a clear visual distinction between advisory work, engineering work, and demand-generation work.
- Use motion to explain systems and relationships, not merely to decorate the page.

## 8. UX Opportunities

The redesign should consider improving:

- The hierarchy between strategy, engineering, and implementation
- The explanation of the ideal client
- The distinction between consulting and engineering services
- The credibility and presentation of case studies
- Human and team storytelling
- The explanation of what happens after booking
- The difference between a free strategy session and a paid engagement
- The relationship between business problems and specific services
- The conversion path from problem recognition to booking
- The presentation of technology partners
- Proof points, testimonials, and client credibility

A useful structure could organize the site around visitor problems first, then map those problems to services and outcomes.

## 9. Content and Credibility Questions to Resolve

Before final design and copy approval, the team should clarify:

- Are the case studies public client work, anonymized work, or illustrative examples?
- Can client names, logos, testimonials, or more detailed evidence be shown?
- Is the company primarily serving Nigeria, the United States, or global clients?
- Should the booking page say “free,” “complimentary,” or “assessment session”?
- Is the $2,500 estimated value claim supported by a specific deliverable?
- Which industries are the strongest commercial focus rather than simply possible areas of work?
- What team members should be featured?
- What is the typical engagement size, duration, and starting price?
- What happens after someone submits the contact form or books a session?

These answers will materially affect the information hierarchy and the level of proof required in the new design.

## 12. Inspiration Analysis

The supplied references point toward a more human, editorial, and premium direction than the current dashboard-heavy interface. They should be treated as sources of design principles and interaction patterns, not as layouts or visual assets to reproduce.

### Shared patterns across the references

- A strong first viewport with one clear promise and one dominant visual.
- Human subjects used to make professional services feel trustworthy and approachable.
- Large rounded containers that create a polished, framed composition.
- Clear separation between hero, proof, services, work examples, testimonials, and conversion sections.
- Generous whitespace around short, high-confidence copy.
- Small labels and eyebrow text used to establish hierarchy.
- Bold metric cards and outcome-led proof points.
- Repeated use of modular cards, grids, and bento-style compositions.
- Dark sections used selectively for emphasis rather than as the entire experience.
- Warm yellow, gold, teal, or blue accents reserved for CTAs, active states, and meaningful highlights.
- Alternating image and text sections that create a natural editorial rhythm.
- Rounded, high-contrast CTA buttons with directional arrow icons.
- Footer layouts that combine brand statement, navigation, contact details, and a final conversion action.

### Reference-specific observations

#### Yellow electrical services reference

Useful concepts:

- A confident, high-energy hero with a clear service promise.
- A human professional as the visual anchor.
- Curved or flowing transitions between major sections.
- Three simple “what we do” cards immediately after the hero.
- Small trust badges and service guarantees near the first CTA.
- A dark, structured footer that closes the experience clearly.

How to translate it for SuperTelque:

- Replace the electrician with a founder, revenue leader, or SuperTelque operator image.
- Use the hero visual to show a real operating system, team interaction, or carefully composed technical artifact.
- Use three cards for RevOps, AI engineering, and demand systems.
- Turn the trust badges into concise proof such as “Senior operator-led,” “Built around your stack,” and “Measured against revenue outcomes.”

Do not copy the loud yellow treatment directly. SuperTelque needs more restraint and should use gold as a precise signal rather than a full-page background.

#### Dark legal services reference

Useful concepts:

- A contained, cinematic hero panel with a dark premium surface.
- A compact credibility strip with metrics directly below the hero.
- A clear “about” statement before the service catalogue.
- Horizontal or draggable service cards for multiple offerings.
- Case work presented as a structured list rather than only as large cards.

How to translate it for SuperTelque:

- Use a dark framed hero for the homepage or Services page.
- Place proof such as forecast accuracy, response time, selling-time lift, and pipeline impact below the opening statement.
- Present the three service pillars as a controlled horizontal system map or card rail.
- Give case studies a more editorial index with industry, challenge, outcome, and engagement type.

#### Real estate reference

Useful concepts:

- A large photographic hero with a dark overlay and one focused CTA.
- Overlapping white and colored panels that create depth.
- Alternating sections with text, image, and compact data controls.
- Person-focused cards alongside product or property imagery.
- A strong sense of movement through offset blocks and asymmetrical composition.

How to translate it for SuperTelque:

- Use overlapping visual panels to explain how data, systems, and teams connect.
- Pair an operator portrait or team image with a visualized pipeline or workflow artifact.
- Use offset case-study layouts rather than a repeated three-column card grid.
- Let each service have a distinct visual “world” while retaining one shared design system.

#### Light business consulting reference

Useful concepts:

- A light, spacious canvas with a calm executive tone.
- A clear hero with a professional human subject.
- Four compact service cards with one highlighted or dark featured card.
- A mix of text blocks, portrait cards, image cards, and proof modules.
- A bento layout that makes a long page scannable.

How to translate it for SuperTelque:

- Use a light editorial mode for About, Services, or Case Studies pages.
- Create a “revenue friction” bento section with cards for data quality, forecasting, routing, automation, and reporting.
- Use one dark featured service or proof card to create visual rhythm.
- Pair technical language with more human imagery so the company does not feel like an anonymous implementation shop.

#### Healthcare and wellness reference

Useful concepts:

- A calm blue hero with an older human subject and an immediately reassuring message.
- Alternating sections that combine editorial copy with proof cards.
- Expert profiles and testimonial modules.
- A dark testimonial band that breaks up a light page.
- A dedicated article or insights section.
- A contact section that pairs a form with a contextual image.

How to translate it for SuperTelque:

- Add a visible “who you work with” or team section to establish human credibility.
- Use client quotes, operator commentary, or outcome testimonials where available.
- Consider an insights section for RevOps, AI systems, and GTM architecture content.
- Design Contact as a reassuring, low-friction conversation rather than a bare form.

#### Yellow employee-volunteering reference

Useful concepts:

- A bold, memorable brand color used as the opening scene.
- Playful, colorful service cards with simple icons.
- Product imagery embedded directly into the narrative.
- A logo or customer strip after the first explanatory section.
- A dark product/reporting section for contrast.

How to translate it for SuperTelque:

- Use a distinctive accent color or accent band to make the brand more recognizable.
- Show workflow screens, CRM views, or system diagrams as tangible artifacts, not abstract decoration.
- Add a restrained client or technology ecosystem strip after the first value section.
- Use a dark “inside the system” section to reveal technical depth.

The playful illustration style itself is not the right fit for SuperTelque. The useful lesson is the confidence, clarity, and willingness to use a memorable accent.

#### Blue AI and strategy consulting reference

Useful concepts:

- A centered, high-level statement about the future of the category.
- Floating data cards that make abstract consulting services tangible.
- A prominent client or performance metric strip.
- A strong “about us” statement supported by measurable proof.
- Bright blue used as an optimistic, intelligent brand field.

How to translate it for SuperTelque:

- Represent the revenue system as a living network of connected data, decisions, and actions.
- Use floating cards for pipeline, conversion, forecast confidence, and automation metrics.
- Make the technology ecosystem feel like an engineered stack rather than a logo ticker.
- Keep metrics tied to business outcomes and clearly label whether they are examples, benchmarks, or client results.

Avoid generic floating UI cards that look like a SaaS template. Every card should explain a real SuperTelque concept.

## 13. Recommended New Visual System

### Overall direction

The best synthesis of the references is:

> A human-led, editorial consulting site with the precision of a revenue operating system.

The experience should combine:

- The warmth and confidence of the yellow service-business reference
- The authority and framing of the dark legal reference
- The layered composition of the real-estate reference
- The modular clarity of the consulting reference
- The proof and testimonial rhythm of the healthcare reference
- The memorable color confidence of the volunteering reference
- The intelligent systems visualization of the AI consulting reference

### Suggested palette

- Warm off-white or soft stone as the primary light canvas
- Deep ink or blue-black for authority sections
- A single signature gold, amber, or citrus accent for primary actions
- Controlled electric blue or teal for system states and technical emphasis
- Soft gray-blue surfaces for secondary panels

The palette should feel warmer and more editorial than the existing pure-white, navy, and neon-accent system. Avoid using every reference color at once. Color should come from a small, deliberate set of named surfaces and accents rather than gradients layered across every section.

### Color and card rules

- Use color confidently on individual cards and feature panels.
- Give service cards distinct but harmonious surface colors, such as soft yellow, pale blue, muted teal, warm coral, or lavender.
- Keep card backgrounds mostly flat or very subtly textured.
- Reserve gradients for rare focal moments, if they are needed at all. Do not use gradients as a default background treatment.
- Ensure every color has a purpose: categorizing a service, highlighting a result, signaling an interaction, or creating rhythm between sections.
- Keep body text and controls high-contrast against colored surfaces.
- Do not add decorative numbers such as `01`, `02`, or `03` to cards unless the number communicates a real sequence or process step.
- Let card titles, icons, imagery, and short descriptions create hierarchy instead of serial numbering.

### Suggested typography

- A distinctive display sans or contemporary grotesk for major headlines
- A highly readable neutral sans for body copy
- Monospace reserved for system labels, metrics, stack details, and technical metadata
- Short headlines with strong line breaks and generous surrounding space

The typography should feel more like a premium strategy firm than a developer tool.

### Suggested layout language

- Wide framed hero panels with a strong radius, especially on desktop
- Image-led hero compositions, with photography or a carefully art-directed visual integrated into the background or main visual field
- Full-width section bands alternating between light and dark modes
- Asymmetric two-column compositions
- Offset image and proof cards
- Bento grids for capabilities and outcomes
- Horizontal case-study or service rails where multiple items need comparison
- Strong vertical spacing and clear section transitions
- Consistent, moderate corner radii instead of excessive pill-shaped UI

### Image direction

Images should be a primary part of the storytelling, not an occasional decoration.

- Use a strong hero image or art-directed background that makes the business feel human and credible.
- Prefer photographs of operators, founders, revenue teams, working sessions, or real system artifacts over generic abstract technology imagery.
- Use images in service sections, case studies, About content, testimonials, and the Contact experience where they add context.
- Combine photography with colored panels, cropped frames, and layered cards to create depth without relying on gradients.
- Choose images with enough negative space for readable text and a clear focal point.
- Use consistent image treatment across the site so the imagery feels like one brand system.
- Avoid stock imagery that looks staged, overly corporate, or disconnected from revenue operations.

### Card direction

Cards should feel like designed editorial objects, not repeated dashboard containers.

- Use a varied but controlled palette across card groups.
- Allow some cards to be image-led, some text-led, and some metric-led.
- Use cards to create rhythm through size, color, and composition rather than repeated borders.
- Keep card copy short and scannable.
- Use icons sparingly and only when they clarify the category or action.
- Remove decorative card numbering from services, capabilities, industries, and proof modules.
- Use a dark or strongly colored card only when it creates a meaningful focal point.
- Keep shadows soft and depth intentional.

## 14. Proposed Homepage Experience

The inspiration suggests a revised homepage sequence:

- **Hero:** Revenue systems that give leadership a clearer view of growth, with a human or team-led background image and one primary booking CTA.
- **Immediate proof strip:** A small set of real outcomes, client categories, or operating principles.
- **What SuperTelque solves:** Colorful cards for forecasting, CRM chaos, lead routing, manual work, and AI adoption.
- **Three service pillars:** RevOps, AI engineering, and demand systems, each with a distinct color and image treatment.
- **How the system works:** Audit, architect, automate, accelerate, shown as a connected flow rather than four isolated numbered cards.
- **Selected case work:** Editorial case-study blocks with challenge, intervention, image or system artifact, and measurable outcome.
- **Human credibility:** Team or operator section, client quote, or founder/CRO perspective.
- **Technology ecosystem:** A quiet, well-designed stack section supporting the business story.
- **Strategy session CTA:** Explain exactly what happens in 45 minutes and why it is valuable.
- **Dark footer:** Navigation, regions, standards, social links, and contact information.

## 15. Motion and Interaction Direction

Use motion to make systems understandable:

- Cards can enter in a staggered editorial sequence.
- Workflow lines can show data moving from source to decision to action.
- Metrics can count or update only when they represent a meaningful state change.
- Case studies can use a restrained horizontal rail or scroll-linked reveal.
- Service cards can expand to reveal a deeper operating model.
- Hero visuals can have subtle parallax or layered movement.
- The booking flow should use clear transitions between date, time, and details.

Avoid:

- Constant dashboard animation
- Excessive card flipping
- Decorative parallax with no explanatory purpose
- Motion that delays access to the primary CTA
- Interactions that make content inaccessible on mobile or for reduced-motion users

## 16. Direct Design Recommendation

The redesign should not reproduce any single reference. It should use the references to establish a new SuperTelque language:

> Warm human authority on the outside, rigorous revenue engineering underneath.

That means the first impression should be approachable, clear, and premium. As the visitor continues, the site should progressively reveal the technical depth: workflows, architecture, data models, integrations, and measurable outcomes.

## 17. Priority Preferences From Inspiration Review

The most important visual preferences to carry into the redesign are:

- Make the hero image-led, using a strong background or primary visual rather than a dashboard as the only focal point.
- Use colorful cards as a defining part of the interface. The colors should feel intentional, coordinated, and tied to content categories.
- Keep gradients rare. Prefer well-chosen flat colors, soft surfaces, photography, and composition to create visual richness.
- Remove unnecessary decorative numbering from cards. Number content only when it represents a real process order.
- Use imagery throughout the page: hero, service explanations, case studies, About, testimonials, and contact.
- Keep the overall result clean and premium by balancing colorful components with generous whitespace and restrained typography.
